About and method

Maine Ledger is an independent weekly brief on Maine’s county jails, state prisons and criminal courts. Each entry states what a public document says and links to the document.

Who publishes it

Maine Ledger is edited independently in Maine.

It takes no funding from political parties, campaigns, or vendors to correctional systems, and endorses no candidate or ballot question.

What counts as a record

A record is a document produced by a public body or a court: meeting minutes and packets, statutes and bills, adopted rules, budgets, audits, inspection reports, court orders, and agency data reports. Documents are preferred over characterizations.

How entries are chosen

  • An entry needs a document. If the document is not yet available, the entry says so.
  • Four to six entries a week, newest first by the date on the document.
  • No commentary. An entry reports what changed and what the document says.

How to read an entry

  • Date is the date on the document, not the date we wrote about it.
  • Place is the body that produced the record: a county, the State House, a state department.
  • The card on the right is the record. The line at its foot names the site it opens on, so you can see it is the government’s own copy before you click.
  • Reporting under an entry links to news coverage of the same record, for context. The record is the source; the coverage is not.
  • LD means Legislative Document, Maine’s bill number. MDOC is the Maine Department of Corrections.

Following the Ledger

Each issue is posted on the home page and keeps a permanent address, such as /issue/1, so a shared link always points at the issue it came from. Follow new issues and reports by RSS, or search everything published so far.

How reporting is filed

Each piece is filed by topic, format, system and facility, and by bill where one applies. Each label answers a different question.

  • Topic is what it is about, one per piece: Conditions and Safety; Health and Treatment; Operations and Daily Life, which covers food, commissary, phones, mail, visits and programs; Money and Contracts; Law and Policy; Courts and Pretrial; Release and Reentry. Browse by topic.
  • Format is what kind of piece it is: news, investigation, data, explainer or source document.
  • Facility. Each jail and prison has one page collecting every entry, report and record about it. Pages use the official name; the names locals and records use, such as Windham for the Maine Correctional Center, are listed on the page.
  • Bill. Each bill has one page, named by LD number and Legislature, because LD numbers restart every two years.
  • System is who holds the person: a county jail, the state prison system, the federal Bureau of Prisons, juvenile custody or community supervision.
  • Outside Maine. Facilities in other states are listed when people from Maine are held there.

Corrections

Corrections run at the top of the next issue, set at the same size as the error, and are noted on the original entry. Issues are not otherwise edited after publication.
