Aroostook County Jail: perfect scores, unsafe building

Five straight 100 percent state inspections. No fire suppression. A capacity that rose as the jail filled, a deficit that tripled, a death the record barely notes, and a design firm the county’s own interview team did not pick.

The Aroostook County Jail has scored 100 percent on five straight state inspections. In 2024, federal consultants walked the same building in Houlton and found it has no fire suppression system and no way to clear smoke.

Both findings are on the public record. A perfect score, the consultants wrote, “is not atypical when establishing minimum standards and working with agencies to find operational means to overcome physical plant deficiencies.”

01Perfect scores, unsafe building

The NIC consultants, July 2024: “numerous fire and life safety issues that simply cannot be addressed … in a building of this vintage.” The exam room is “woefully too small”; visitation is not ADA accessible. They did not fault the staff. “To expect a jail … to function for 50 years, let alone 135 years is unrealistic.”

02A capacity number that kept moving

Rated capacity nearly doubled since 1991; the building did not. After a 2013 overcrowding citation, the number rose from 72 to 123. The NIC put functional capacity at 94.

03A death the record barely notes

The MDOC 2021 Report on Jails lists a death review for “Aroostook County Jail,” dated 05/25/21. That is all it says. Searches of five Maine news outlets found no story. The review has not been published.

04Where the jail sits

Houlton is an hour from Presque Isle; consultants found northern arrests “regularly not made” to avoid the trip. In 2024 a justice found “no rostered attorneys available” in the county.

05The price tag and the design firm

Estimates run $85 to $120 million. The interview team polled 9–0 for WBRC. Thirteen days later, commissioners hired SMRT, citing “lower pre-referendum costs.” Eight sites were offered; a bond vote could come in 2027.

06A growing deficit

The jail fund has ended in the red in six of seven audited years. The auditor did not audit the Inmate Benefit Fund for five years.
