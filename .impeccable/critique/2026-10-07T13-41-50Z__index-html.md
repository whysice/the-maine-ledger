## Design Specificity Verdict

**Review.** The structure is authored and the skin is category-default. Authored: the four-column row (date, place, entry, record) ending in a document card with type, title, date and pin cite; the "What changed since № 11" before-and-after panel; the issue strip that counts records cited; the footer policy lines; the 404 that reads "No entry at this address". Default: Spectral with JetBrains Mono on cream #f6f4ee with hairline rules and one moss accent #2e5a3c, the standard independent-newsletter kit, plus giant stat numerals and a "read time" cell. Nothing visual says Maine, and nothing separates jails from prisons from courts, the three beats in the tagline. A record-based brief on Ohio school boards could take this page unchanged.

**Detector.** Front page: 0 findings. Archive, thanks and 404: 0. The two report pages carry 2 findings each, both true hits and both low impact: a thick accent left border on the summary box (story.html line 115, petersburg.html line 116; the box is hidden on load) and a width transition on the 2px audio progress bar (lines 114 and 115).

**Overlays.** None. The in-page overlay needs a detector server your Chrome can reach, and mine runs in a cloud workspace it cannot. Measured evidence was taken in the page instead: every text pair passes contrast (lowest 6.56:1), no horizontal overflow at 375px or 320px, 0 console errors, zero images, about 177 KB cold load of which about 148 KB is fonts.

## Overall Impression

The row is the argument: claim on the left, document on the right, "check me" built into the layout. Then the document will not open. The biggest opportunity is making the record the largest, most clickable thing in each row instead of the smallest.

## What's Working

- **The row grammar.** A lawmaker's staffer can see at a glance that every claim has a paper behind it, with a page and item number on the Aroostook entry.
- **"What changed since № 11."** Three before-and-after lines answer the weekly reader's only question. This is the most ownable idea on the page.
- **Voice in the small places.** "Set at the same size as the error" and "Documents are preferred over characterizations" do more for trust than any visual. Focus ring is visible (7.22:1) and body contrast is strong.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The record cannot be opened.**
- What: 21 of the 37 links on the front page are href="#": all 4 document cards, all 16 county links, and Unsubscribe. Clicking one scrolls to the masthead.
- Why it matters: the tagline is "by the record" and the lead says each row carries "the document itself". The two long reports link out heavily; the front page is the weakest-sourced page on the site.
- Fix: link each card to the official URL and make the whole card the target. Where no document is ready, print "document pending" as plain text, not a link. Pull the county links until county views exist. Remove Unsubscribe from the web footer; it belongs in the email.
- Where: index.html lines 116, 121, 126, 131, 144, 169.
- Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] Sample content is published as the sent record.**
- What: the page says "ALL 12 ISSUES", the archive says "every issue, as sent", counties show year-to-date entry counts, and issues 8 to 11 link to an archive where they cannot be opened. The site is indexable (robots.txt allows all, no noindex). I could not verify which of the four № 12 entries are real.
- Why it matters: accountability outlets rarely get discredited on their reporting. They get discredited on one thing on the page that is not true, and an agency press office looks for exactly that. An archive that claims issues nobody received is that thing.
- Fix: one of two. Keep it a design preview (noindex, a one-line "sample entries" banner) until launch, or renumber to the real № 1 and show only entries that exist.
- Where: index.html lines 142 to 156; archive.html lines 6 and 59; robots.txt.
- Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P1] No publisher, no method, and a personal Gmail as the corrections desk.**
- What: the front page never names who edits it. "About the mark" opens identity.html, an internal brand sheet. Corrections and tips both go to a personal Gmail handle on a netlify.app address.
- Why it matters: the first question from a staffer deciding whether to forward this is "who is this". What gets forwarded has a named editor, a stated method and a corrections log.
- Fix: one About and Method page (editor, location, what counts as a record, how entries are chosen, funding, running corrections log), linked in nav and footer; "Edited by" in the issue strip; a role address for corrections.
- Where: index.html lines 89 to 94 and 165 to 170; identity.html.
- Suggested command: /impeccable shape, then /impeccable clarify

**[P1] Phone readers lose the nav and the labels.**
- What (confirmed in a 375px render): under 560px the header keeps only Subscribe; This week, By county and Archive vanish with no replacement. Under 980px the column heads are hidden, so date, place and record type stack unlabeled. The email field is 15px, which makes iOS Safari zoom on focus, and it and the button are 40px tall. The page runs 4,281px long at 375px.
- Fix: keep a compact text nav at every width; 16px input, 44px controls; make the whole document card and county row the tap target; inline labels on stacked rows.
- Where: index.html lines 26 and 56; ledger.css lines 15, 17, 20.
- Suggested command: /impeccable adapt

**[P2] Type scale and accent color editorialize on a page that promises no commentary.**
- What: the loudest element is a 76px August prison count, larger than the masthead (56px) and the headline (40px), and "215" is set in accent green. Green on "Enacted", "Closed" and "+$4M" reads as approval. The strip says "4 documents" while the numerals cite a fifth, unlinked source. Rows are out of date order. Only one of four cards has a pin cite.
- Fix: cut the numerals to about 40px under a "Standing counts" label with a linked, dated source; reserve green for links and focus, and set changes in ink weight instead; sort rows by date or state the rule; give every card a page or section cite.
- Where: index.html lines 46, 58 to 62, 113 to 137.
- Suggested command: /impeccable typeset, then /impeccable quieter

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-time public reader).** Follows the lead sentence, hits "LD 1962", "MDOC" and "JAIL $" with no gloss, clicks "Aroostook County Commissioners, regular meeting", lands at the masthead, and decides the site is broken. Looks for who runs it and finds color swatches.

**Sam (keyboard and screen reader).** No h1, no main landmark, no skip link; five tab stops before content. The ledger is tabular data built from spans, so cells are read without column names. Sixteen county links are announced without their counts and go nowhere. No visited color on a page whose job is tracking which documents you have opened. The input border is 1.46:1 against the page, under the 3:1 bar for control boundaries.

**Casey (phone, one hand).** Header offers only Subscribe. Scrolls four stacked entries and two giant numerals to reach the reports, the only fully sourced content. The input zooms on focus. 29 of 36 tap targets are under 44px tall.

## Minor Observations

- The service worker serves ledger.css cache-first under the name maine-ledger-v3, so style fixes will not reach returning readers until that name changes. It also caches 404 responses as pages.
- The _ds/broadsheet folder and ds-base.js (a second design system: Source Serif 4, cyan, magenta) are referenced by no page. Dead weight in the repo.
- Google Fonts loads through an @import inside ledger.css, a chained blocking request. JetBrains Mono 500 is requested and never used.
- The front page has no print styles and does not link to print.html, though a print edition exists.
- email.html still carries "P.O. Box 000".
- The dash in the sanctions entry lands on "or after a finding of not guilty" and reads as emphasis.
- 404 and thanks pages render the tagline cramped (missing letter-spacing reset); the archive totals row draws stray vertical rules.
- No dark scheme; input text computes to pure black, not the ink token; the manifest is served as octet-stream.
- Five running-text sizes (14, 15, 16, 17px plus 12px mono) muddy the scale.
